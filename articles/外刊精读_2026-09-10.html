若无城市树木，城市"热岛"热度将翻倍

Combining data from 8,919 large cities globally, a study highlights the extent to which trees cool cities — and the urgent need to green vulnerable neighbourhoods
来源 The Nature Conservancy（原稿全文）作者 TNC Newsroom日期 原发 2026-05-05难度 较高

📌 本文为外刊原文：The Nature Conservancy（大自然保护协会）新闻稿原文，未做改写，仅作生词标注与拆解。原文载于 Nature.org Newsroom，基于 Nature Communications 论文（McDonald et al., 2026）。

中文导读

如果没有城市树木，全球城市的"热岛"热度将翻倍。由大自然保护协会（TNC）牵头、发表于《自然·通讯》的这项史无前例的研究，汇集了全球近 9000 个大城市、约 36 亿人口的数据，发现树冠目前可抵消近一半（约 48.6%）的城市热岛效应，全球逾 2 亿城市居民因此享受到比无树情景低 0.5°C 以上的气温。但研究也揭示了一个鲜明的不公平：树木的降温作用目前集中在高收入国家、湿润气候和郊区——恰恰是需求最不紧迫的地方；到 2050 年，现有及未来的树冠最多也只能抵消 9%–10% 的气候升温。科学家呼吁，扩大树冠覆盖固然关键，但仅靠树木远远不够，人类还需多种策略适应更热的世界，并尽快减少温室气体排放。

一、原文精读

以下为 The Nature Conservancy 新闻稿英文原文（全文）。鼠标悬停在带 蓝色虚线下划线 的生词上，即可查看词性和释义。

【1】The world's 'urban heat islands' (UHIs) would be twice as hot without the cooling effect trees have on superheated cityscapes, according to an unprecedented study that also highlights stark inequalities in access to this powerful form of nature-based air conditioning.
【2】Led by The Nature Conservancy (TNC) and published in Nature Communications, the paper pools data from nearly 9,000 large cities worldwide – collectively home to ~3.6 billion people – to find that tree cover currently mitigates nearly half (~48.6%) of the UHI effect that occurs when man-made surfaces like roads, buildings and car parks absorb and release heat from the sun, causing urban areas to heat up more than surrounding rural regions.
【3】Over 200 million city-dwellers globally, the study finds, have trees to thank for ambient air temperatures more than 0.5°C lower than would otherwise be the case in their neighbourhoods – a highly significant contrast when extrapolated across the thousands of cities, billions of citizens and increasingly life-threatening temperatures concerned.
【4】Commenting on the study's significance, Rob McDonald, TNC's lead global scientist for both nature-based solutions and for its European regional team, said, "By combining datasets showing air temperatures at 1km scale with high-resolution land cover maps, we've succeeded for the first time in producing tangible estimates for a natural cooling effect that's long been accepted but never comprehensively measured until now."
【5】Co-authored by scientists from Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL), SUNY ESF and Western Sydney University, the study also finds that the cooling effect of trees is currently concentrated in places where the need is less acute: high-income countries, humid climates and suburban neighbourhoods.
【6】Compounding this inequality is the study's projection that current and future tree cover will only be able to mitigate 9-10% of the increase in temperatures caused by climate change that is anticipated by 2050. The maximum possible tree planting scenario increases this to around 20%, making it doubly urgent that governments commit to increasing tree canopy cover across their most densely settled and low-income urban areas while there's still time to allow full canopy growth.
【7】Adding further urgency, Rob McDonald said, "While expanding tree cover – particularly in urban areas that don't have enough right now – is an essential part of adapting to rising temperatures, our study suggests that trees by themselves won't be enough. Humanity will need to use multiple strategies to adapt to a hotter world, and we desperately need to reduce greenhouse gas pollution to avoid committing ourselves to catastrophically high temperatures."
【8】TC Chakraborty, Earth Scientist at PNNL, who led the remote sensing analysis for the study, said: "Most global studies rely on satellite land surface temperature, which significantly overstates the cooling benefits of trees. But air temperature alone doesn't tell the whole story. By modeling Wet Bulb Globe Temperature – a metric that also factors in humidity, wind, and solar radiation – at the local scale for a small subset of cities, we found that trees reduce actual human heat stress by an average of three times more than air temperature suggests. This gives us a much more accurate, physiologically relevant estimate of how trees can protect urban residents during hot, sunny, summer days."
【9】"It's increasingly common to see stark temperature differences between neighbourhoods in the same city, driven by unequal amounts of tree canopy," added Johnny Quispe, director of cities programs at TNC. "The impacts of extreme heat often affect the most vulnerable communities. Investing in urban forestry yields cooler streets, cleaner air and more resilient communities for everyone."

段落主旨

  1. 若无树木降温，城市"热岛"热度将翻倍；研究同时揭示降温权益的鲜明不平等。
  2. 研究汇集近 9000 个大城市数据，发现树冠可抵消近半（48.6%）热岛效应。
  3. 全球逾 2 亿城市居民受益于树木，气温比无树情景低 0.5°C 以上。
  4. 麦当劳：首次结合 1km 气温数据与高分辨率土地覆盖图，量化自然降温效应。
  5. 树木降温目前集中在高收入国家、湿润气候和郊区——需求最不紧迫之处。
  6. 到 2050 年树冠仅能抵消 9–10% 升温，最多植树情景也仅约 20%。
  7. 仅靠树木不够，还需多种策略适应更热世界，并亟需减少温室气体排放。
  8. 查克拉博蒂：用湿球黑球温度测得实际热应激缓解是气温数据的三倍。
  9. 街区温差源于树冠不均；投资城市林业带来更凉爽、更清洁、更有韧性的社区。

三、长难句分析（三步拆解法）

The world's 'urban heat islands' (UHIs) would be twice as hot without the cooling effect trees have on superheated cityscapes, according to an unprecedented study that also highlights stark inequalities in access to this powerful form of nature-based air conditioning.
主干：The world's 'urban heat islands' (UHIs) would be twice as hot（主语 + would be + 表语）
条件状语（without + 名词短语）：without the cooling effect trees have on superheated cityscapes（内含省略关系词的定语从句 trees have on superheated cityscapes，修饰 effect）

考点：虚拟语气 would be（without 作含蓄条件）；省略关系词的定语从句；that 引导定语从句

考点：过去分词作状语；that 宾语从句与定语从句的区分；when 时间状语从句；现在分词作结果状语
译文：这项由大自然保护协会（TNC）牵头、发表于《自然·通讯》的研究，汇集了全球近 9000 个大城市（约 36 亿人口）的数据，发现树冠目前可抵消近一半（约 48.6%）的热岛效应——当道路、建筑和停车场等人造表面吸收并释放太阳热量、使城市比周边乡村升温更高时，这一效应就会显现。

四、全文大意

这篇大自然保护协会（TNC）的新闻稿报道了一项发表于《自然·通讯》的重大研究：如果没有城市树木的降温作用，全球城市的"热岛"热度将翻倍。研究汇集了近 9000 个大城市、约 36 亿人口的数据，发现树冠目前可抵消近一半（约 48.6%）的城市热岛效应，全球逾 2 亿城市居民因此享受到比无树情景低 0.5°C 以上的气温。然而研究也揭示了一个鲜明的不平等：树木的降温作用目前集中在高收入国家、湿润气候和郊区——恰恰是降温需求最不紧迫的地方；更糟的是，到 2050 年，现有及未来的树冠最多只能抵消 9%–10% 的气候升温，即使是最激进的植树情景也仅约 20%。科学家因此强调，扩大树冠覆盖固然重要，但仅靠树木远远不够，人类必须采用多种策略适应更热的世界，并尽快减少温室气体排放。研究还改进了测量方法——用"湿球黑球温度"替代卫星地表温度，发现树木对人体的实际降温（热应激缓解）是气温数据的三倍。最终，研究者呼吁投资城市林业，让最脆弱的社区也能享有更凉爽的街道、更清洁的空气和更有韧性的未来。
